10 · SYNTHESIS

Four conclusions survive the cross-check

01

Scale without mix control destroys quality

Evidence: May absorbed 13,769 contacts—33.43% of the campaign—but converted at only 6.43%. Telephone carried 8,251 of those contacts, while cellular represented 40.08% of the month.

The operational issue is not insufficient volume; it is whether volume is being placed into combinations of channel, audience and history that can sustain response.

DECISION Diagnose May at channel × job × prior-outcome level before adding or reallocating contact capacity.
02

Cellular is the strongest hypothesis—not yet the answer

Evidence: Cellular converted at 14.74% versus 5.23% for telephone and generated 83.04% of observed conversions from 63.47% of contacts.

The gap is commercially material, but audience selection, month, prior history and contact pressure can all contribute to it.

DECISION Use matched cohorts or a randomized holdout; scale only the incremental lift that survives the test.
03

Audience strategy needs separate precision and scale lanes

Evidence: Students converted at 31.43% across 875 contacts and retirees at 25.23% across 1,720; admin. delivered a lower 12.97% rate but across 10,422 contacts.

High-rate niche groups support precise treatment, while large cohorts determine total conversion opportunity and operating workload.

DECISION Prioritize by expected conversions and reachable base, retaining the 500-contact credibility guardrail.
04

Contact pressure should become an explicit policy

Evidence: Conversion fell from 13.04% at one contact to 11.22% at two–three, 8.68% at four–five and 3.11% at 11+. The first two bands contain 81.5% of contacts.

The pattern is consistent with diminishing productivity, but harder-to-convert customers also accumulate more attempts.

DECISION Test a soft intervention after three contacts and measure incremental conversions plus contacts avoided.

11 · MANAGEMENT ACTIONS

Turn the evidence into a measured test cadence

NOW · DIAGNOSE

Explain the May volume-quality gap

Produce a decomposition of May contacts and conversions by channel, job and prior outcome. The purpose is to locate which combinations absorbed volume without producing proportionate subscriptions.

Evidence trigger: 33.43% of all contacts, 6.43% conversion and only 40.08% cellular share in May.
  • Owner: Campaign analytics
  • Decision metric: conversions per 1,000 contacts
  • Exit criterion: identify cohorts that explain most of the shortfall
NEXT · EXPERIMENT

Estimate incremental channel lift

Randomize operationally eligible customers between cellular and telephone under the same timing and audience rules. Do not use the observed 2.8× ratio as the expected causal effect.

Evidence trigger: 14.74% cellular conversion versus 5.23% telephone conversion.
  • Owner: Channel operations
  • Decision metric: incremental conversion and cost per lift
  • Scale rule: expand only if the holdout confirms improvement
SEGMENT · PRIORITIZE

Build precision and scale queues

Create one queue for high-rate, credible niche groups and another for large operational cohorts. This prevents a small attractive rate from displacing a larger source of total conversions.

Evidence trigger: student 31.43% at n=875; admin. 12.97% at n=10,422.
  • Owner: CRM strategy
  • Decision metric: expected conversions = reach × rate
  • Guardrail: 500+ contacts for headline ranking
TEST · GOVERN

Introduce a soft contact intervention

After three campaign contacts, test a pause, alternate treatment or escalation rule while retaining a randomized control group. Review the policy regularly rather than treating one test as permanent truth.

Evidence trigger: 13.04% at one contact, falling to 8.68% at four–five and 3.11% at 11+.
  • Owner: Contact policy
  • Decision metric: conversions and contacts avoided
  • Guardrail: separate fatigue from selection effects
