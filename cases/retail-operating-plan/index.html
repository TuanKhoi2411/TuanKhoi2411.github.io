01 / FP&A · RETAIL

A retail plan built from 1.07M transactions

How should a seasonal retailer set a useful run-rate, protect peak months, and monitor revenue quality?

DECISION BRIEF

Clean the transaction layer first, then separate recurring run-rate, seasonality, geography, and cancellations before planning actions.

Scope
2 years of transactions
Toolkit
Python · Power Query · Excel · Scenario design
£20.97Mclean observed sales
40,077unique orders
1.8%cancelled line rate

THE CHALLENGE

Transactions are not yet a plan.

The source contains more than one million line items across two years. Before asking about growth, the analysis has to distinguish sales from cancellations, remove non-positive quantities and prices, and preserve a direct line back to the invoice.

The management need is a reliable seasonal baseline: what is recurring, what is concentrated, and where should a planner set guardrails?

01 / QUALITY1.8%

Cancellation lines need their own control.

Treating cancellations as ordinary negative sales obscures demand and return behavior.

02 / CONCENTRATION85.2%

The United Kingdom drives observed sales.

International growth should be planned as a separate lane, not blended into the core run-rate.

03 / CAPACITYNov

Peak readiness matters more than a flat average.

Inventory, fulfillment, and cash assumptions should flex before the Q4 acceleration.

WORKFLOW

A controlled route from source to action.

  1. 01

    Audit

    Type invoices, dates, quantity and price; flag cancellations and non-positive lines.

  2. 02

    Model

    Build clean revenue, order, customer, country, and monthly views with reconciliation totals.

  3. 03

    Plan

    Translate seasonality and concentration into run-rate, peak-capacity, and cancellation guardrails.

RECOMMENDATION

Protect the peak, plan international separately, and monitor cancellation rate beside revenue—not inside it.

This is a planning interpretation of the public history, not a forecast of the original retailer.
