Should the committee approve an automated sortation line now—and which conditions protect capital if cost or benefit delivery slips?

THE CAPITAL QUESTION

Separate a positive Base case from an approvable investment.

Orion Fulfillment is a fictional distribution operator considering an automated sortation line. The model links a $6.38M installed cost and $0.25M of initial working capital to volume, unit savings, adoption ramp, incremental opex, tax, maintenance investment and residual value.

The objective is not to manufacture a positive NPV. It is to reveal which assumptions create value, how quickly capital returns, where the Downside fails, and which conditions must be evidenced before release.

01 / VALUE$2.87M

The Base case clears the hurdle.

NPV is positive at an 11% WACC and IRR exceeds the 15% Investment Committee threshold.

02 / DOWNSIDE$(4.64M)

Execution slippage destroys value.

Higher installed cost, slower ramp, lower volume and weaker savings turn the Downside NPV negative.

03 / GOVERNANCE9 / 9

Integrity checks pass; assumptions still need owners.

A clean model supports the decision, but commercial quotes and benefit delivery remain management responsibilities.

DECISION ARCHITECTURE

Model. Stress. Govern.

  1. 01

    Model

    Translate installed cost, operating ramp, savings, opex, tax and maintenance into incremental free cash flow and returns.

  2. 02

    Stress

    Compare Downside, Base and Upside outcomes, then test the Base case across savings realization and WACC.

  3. 03

    Govern

    Convert execution risk into fixed-price, acceptance, workforce, benefit-tracking and stop/redesign conditions.

INVESTMENT COMMITTEE READOUT

Approve with stage gates—not as an unconditional capital release.

Secure a fixed-price quote, hold final payment behind acceptance testing, approve the workforce plan, assign Finance ownership for monthly benefit tracking, and stop or redesign if Base NPV falls below zero or a critical control fails.
