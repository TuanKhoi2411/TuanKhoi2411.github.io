02 / PREDICTIVE ANALYTICS

A housing forecast that shows its uncertainty

Can a non-linear model improve valuation estimates without hiding the limits of the data?

DECISION BRIEF

A held-out gradient-boosting model cuts MAE by 67.5% versus a mean baseline, while geography and income remain the dominant signals.

THE CHALLENGE

A prediction is only useful when its error is visible.

The California Housing dataset provides eight numeric drivers for 20,640 census block groups. The task is not just to fit a model; it is to create a defensible benchmark, keep a true holdout, and explain where the estimate may fail.

An 80/20 split with a fixed random seed keeps 4,128 rows untouched until evaluation.

VALIDATION DESIGN

Simple enough to audit.

  1. 01

    Benchmark

    Use the training-set mean as a naive baseline before celebrating model performance.

  2. 02

    Hold out

    Train on 16,512 rows and evaluate once on 4,128 unseen rows.

  3. 03

    Interpret

    Use permutation importance to show which inputs move out-of-sample error most.

MODEL USE

Use it for screening and scenario comparison—not for a final property valuation.

The data comes from the 1990 U.S. Census and caps the target at $500,000, so market recency and upper-tail accuracy are material limits.
